## Goal

Make invoices, payments and idempotency keys live in PostgreSQL with the atomicity that the `InvoiceStore` port documents, so that on the serverless deployment two instances cannot pay one invoice twice, replay a payment wrongly or leave an invoice `paid` with an order that is not. You still have no database: the Supervisor runs the integration tests against a real PostgreSQL and sends back what fails. Write the concurrency tests with threads, barriers and short timeouts so that a deadlock fails fast instead of hanging.

## Requirements

1. **Tables** (`app/modules/invoices/tables.py`, names as given; check constraint names in the migration wrapped in `op.f(...)`):
   - `invoices`: `id integer generated by default as identity` primary key, `number text not null` with the unique index `invoices_number_key`, `status text not null` check (`issued`, `paid`, `void`), `order_id integer not null` referencing `orders(id)` with the unique index `invoices_order_key` (one invoice per order), `order_number text not null`, the customer snapshot `customer_id integer not null`, `customer_name text not null`, `customer_name_search text not null` (the result of `normalize_for_search(customer_name)`, as `customers.name_search`), `customer_tax_id text not null`, `customer_address text`, `subtotal`, `tax` and `total` as `numeric(12,2) not null`, `tax_rate numeric(5,2) not null`, `version integer not null`, `created_at` and `updated_at timestamptz not null`, an index `invoices_created_at_id_idx` on `(created_at desc, id desc)`.
   - `invoice_lines`: `id integer generated by default as identity` primary key (fresh ids, not the order line ids), `invoice_id integer not null` referencing `invoices(id)` on delete cascade, `product_id integer not null`, `sku`, `name`, `quantity integer not null`, `unit_price numeric(12,2) not null`, an index on `invoice_id`.
   - `payments`: `id integer generated by default as identity` primary key, `invoice_id integer not null` referencing `invoices(id)`, `method text not null` check (`card`, `transfer`, `cash`), `status text not null` check (`approved`, `declined`, `failed`), `amount numeric(12,2) not null`, `reference text`, `card_last4 text` with a check that it is four digits when present, `reason text`, `created_at timestamptz not null`, an index on `invoice_id`.
   - `idempotency_keys`: `invoice_id integer not null` referencing `invoices(id)`, `key text not null`, `fingerprint text not null`, `status_code integer not null`, `response jsonb not null`, `created_at timestamptz not null`, primary key `(invoice_id, key)`. While a request is in progress its row holds the placeholders `status_code = 0` and `response = '{}'`; a committed row always holds the final response.
   - The invoice number comes from the sequence `invoice_number_seq` (start 1); gaps are allowed, repeats are not.
   - **The circular reference.** `orders.invoice_id` gets a foreign key to `invoices(id)` created **in the migration only** (`op.create_foreign_key(op.f("fk_orders_invoice_id_invoices"), ...)`, `ondelete="SET NULL"`; the downgrade drops it first). It is not declared in `orders/tables.py`. Together with `invoices.order_id -> orders(id)` it is a cycle, and `ON DELETE SET NULL` is what lets every `replace_all` empty one table before the other.
2. **Migration** `0003` (hand written, with a working `downgrade` that drops the foreign key, the tables and the sequence), and a unit test that the chain is `0001 -> 0002 -> 0003`.
3. **Adapter** (`adapters/postgres.py`): `PostgresInvoiceStore(engine, gateway)` implementing every method of the port (including `find_key`) and every sentence of its docstrings. The business checks are the functions of `invoices/rules.py` (called after the locks, in the documented order); their `ApiError`s propagate unchanged. Not negotiable:
   - One transaction per method. **Lock order** (SPEC section 5): the order row (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`), then the invoice row, then the customer row (`FOR SHARE`) when the customer is needed. **The invoice row is always locked with `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`** (`with_for_update(key_share=True)`), here and in the void of `PostgresOrderStore.transition`: a plain `FOR UPDATE` conflicts with the `FOR KEY SHARE` lock that the foreign key of an `idempotency_keys` or `payments` insert takes on the invoice, and two payments that claimed their keys before locking would deadlock. When a method starts from an invoice id and must lock the order first, read `invoices.order_id` without a lock (it never changes), lock the order, then lock the invoice and read it again. Document all of this in the module docstring.
   - `issue_invoice`: lock the order and the customer; checks in order (version, then `ensure_can_invoice`); `nextval` of the sequence; insert the invoice and its line snapshot (`order_number` and `customer_name_search` included); set the order's `invoice_id` and bump its version (`attach_invoice`). A violation of `invoices_order_key` (a concurrent issue that slipped past the lock) maps to `INVOICE_ALREADY_EXISTS`. The claim insert of `register_payment` on an invoice that vanished (a foreign key violation of `idempotency_keys`) maps to `InvoiceNotFound`; a key is expired when `created_at <= now - 24 hours`.
   - `register_payment`, in one transaction: (a) **claim the key first, before any lock**: delete an expired row of this `(invoice_id, key)` (older than 24 hours with the injected clock), then `INSERT ... ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` with the placeholders; when the insert did nothing, read the existing row: the same fingerprint replays its stored status and body (`replayed=True`), another fingerprint is `IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED` (a concurrent claimant that has not committed makes the insert wait, so the second request sees the committed row, or its own insert succeeds when the first rolled back); (b) lock the order, then the invoice; (c) check the stale `version`, then `ensure_payable` and `ensure_amount`, raising their `ApiError`: the raise rolls the whole transaction back, **including the claim, so a rejected request does not consume the key**; (d) call the gateway while the locks are held; (e) insert the payment row; (f) when approved, set the invoice `paid` (version + 1) and mark the order `paid` (version + 1) on the same connection, with the helper `mark_paid_in` of point 4; (g) call `respond(payment, invoice_after)`, store its `StoredResponse` in the claim row and commit, for approved, declined and failed outcomes alike (a declined or failed attempt **commits** the payment and the key and the method returns an outcome, it does not raise). `find_key` is a plain read of a committed, not expired row.
   - `void_for_order(order_id, now)` standalone: lock the order, then the invoice, void it if it is `issued`. (The production cancel path does not call it: it voids inside `PostgresOrderStore.transition`, point 4.)
   - `list_invoices` (join-free: the snapshot holds the order number and the customer name; `q` matches `lower(number)` or `customer_name_search` by "contains" on the search form, literal with `autoescape=True`), `get_invoice` (with its lines ordered by id and its payments ordered by id), and `replace_all(invoices, payments)`: inside one transaction delete payments, keys, invoice lines and invoices (the foreign key sets `orders.invoice_id` to null), insert the given rows with explicit ids, then set `orders.invoice_id` to the linked invoice for every given invoice **without touching the order's `version` or `updated_at`** (Core `update`, no raw SQL), and move the sequences with schema-qualified raw SQL as in task 018. It accepts the optional keyword `connection` of task 024 only when that task asks for it; for now it opens its own transaction.
   - Timestamps through `as_utc`; money as `Decimal`; the card number and CVC never reach this layer (only `card_last4`); unhandled database errors are not caught and re-raised with their message.
4. **Order store completion** (`orders/adapters/postgres.py`): implement `attach_invoice`, `mark_paid` and `link_invoices` as the port says (one transaction, lock the order row; the first two bump the version, `link_invoices` never does). The standalone port methods are thin wrappers (`engine.begin()`, lock the order, call the helper); the module gets two module-level helpers that take a `Connection` and assume the order row is already locked: `attach_invoice_in(connection, order_id, invoice_id, now)` and `mark_paid_in(connection, order_id, now)`. `PostgresInvoiceStore` calls the helpers on **its own connection** and never calls the port methods inside its transaction (a second connection would wait for the order row that the first holds and self-deadlock until the lock timeout); it reads the order, its lines and the customer with Core queries on `orders`, `order_lines` and `customers`. Also, and make `transition(cancel)` of a `confirmed` order void its `issued` invoice (`status` `void`, `version` + 1) **in the same transaction**, after locking the order and before the stock is returned, with the invoice row locked after the order row and `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`.
5. **Seed and wiring.** The `seed` command loads the 3 invoices, their lines, the 4 payments and the order links, only when `invoices` is empty and after the orders (through `replace_all`, so the seeded orders keep `version` 1 and `updated_at == created_at`); the sequences continue (`FAC-0004`, payment id 5); idempotent. When `settings.database_url` is set, `create_app` builds the PostgreSQL invoice store, the service and the router (task 019 wires them only in memory mode; same names on `app.state`); creating the application still opens no connection.
6. **Tests.**
   - Unit: the migration chain, the table and constraint names (compile the DDL to strings), the wiring, the seed argument handling.
   - Integration: run the existing `InvoiceStore` contract classes against the PostgreSQL store with the engine **without** the pinned search path, plus: the seed twice, and after it `GET`-level proof that orders 3, 4 and 5 have `invoice_id` 1, 2, 3, `version` 1 and `updated_at == created_at`; the constraints (a second invoice for an order, a repeated number, a bad status, `card_last4` with letters, a payment with an unknown method); **two threads paying one invoice with the same key and body** (one payment row, one replay with the same body, the gateway called once); **two threads with different keys, released together by a barrier** (exactly one approval, the other `INVOICE_NOT_PAYABLE`, and **no `DeadlockDetected`**); a key claimed by a request that is then rejected (stale version, amount mismatch) is free again; a declined attempt keeps its key and replays as a 402; the 24-hour expiry; **pay and cancel racing on the same confirmed order, released by a barrier** (both finish within a timeout, no deadlock, and the final states are consistent: either paid and not cancellable, or void and not payable); two concurrent issues for one order (one wins, the other gets `INVOICE_ALREADY_EXISTS`); issue racing with a customer deactivation or a `tax_id` change (the `FOR SHARE` lock makes the snapshot consistent); the snapshot stays unchanged after the customer changes; `replace_all` twice, and `replace_all([], [])` followed by the orders' own `replace_all([], [], rate)` and the customers' (the order the reset of task 024 will use) without a foreign key error; a search with accents on the customer name; the number sequence never repeats under 20 concurrent issues. The race tests pass `expected_version=None`, otherwise the loser gets `VersionConflict` instead of `INVOICE_NOT_PAYABLE` or `INVOICE_ALREADY_EXISTS`. Avoid combining a gateway latency above 5 seconds with a same-key waiter.
7. **Docs.** Extend the "PostgreSQL" section of `backend/README.md`: the new tables, the sequence, the circular reference and why it is `SET NULL`, the lock order with `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, the key claim and the guarantees.
